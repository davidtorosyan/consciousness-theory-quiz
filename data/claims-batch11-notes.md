## Per-theory sources and claims

### 94 — Kriegel's cross-order self-representation
- Page: https://loc.closertotruth.com/theory/kriegel-s-cross-order-self-representation
- **c228 "States represent themselves"** — the self-representational core: a
  state is conscious in virtue of representing itself; peripheral inner
  awareness is internal to the experience. Standalone (deliberately does NOT
  entail c45 "Consciousness needs a higher thought": Kriegel's higher-order
  component is part of the *same* state, not a separate thought).
- **c229 "Integration across representational orders"** — the distinctive
  hypothesis: first-order world-representation + higher-order self-representation
  must be functionally integrated into one state; neither region alone is the
  correlate (Kriegel's derivation: prefrontal–sensory integration required).
  Entails **c228**.
- Theory lists ["c229"] (c228 inherited).
- Family: Materialism. Blurb: "A conscious state is one that represents itself:
  the mind's picture of the world and its picture of that very picture are woven
  into a single state."
- Judgment call: Kriegel is a representationalist about qualitative character,
  but that lineage is left out of the chain to keep the self-representation
  structure primary.

### 95 — Cleeremans's radical plasticity
- Page: https://loc.closertotruth.com/theory/cleeremans-s-learned-consciousness-via-radical-plasticity
- **c230 "Mind represents its own states"** — NEW general higher-order claim:
  consciousness = a higher-order representation of the brain's own first-order
  representations. Standalone. This serves as the parent for Cleeremans and Lau,
  whose higher-order representations are subpersonal/learned rather than
  thought-like (so they should not route through c45).
- **c231 "Consciousness is learned"** — the radical thesis: not an intrinsic
  property of neural states but an acquired capacity; plasticity is the
  generative condition, not a modulator. Standalone.
- **c232 "The brain models itself"** — SOMA: continuous unconscious learning
  redescribes the brain's own activity; learned non-conceptual meta-representations
  track strength/reliability/value; experience requires learning both to *know*
  and to *care*. Entails **c230**, **c231**.
- Theory lists ["c232"] (c230, c231 inherited).
- Family: Materialism. Blurb: "The brain isn't born conscious — it learns to
  be: plasticity teaches it to build a model of its own activity, and that
  self-model is experience."

### 96 — Lau's perceptual reality monitoring
- Page: https://loc.closertotruth.com/theory/lau-s-perceptual-reality-monitoring-theory
- **c233 "Perception is tagged reliable"** — the signature claim: conscious
  perception occurs when a subpersonal higher-order representation tags a
  first-order perceptual representation as "a reliable reflection of the
  external world right now"; blindsight = first-order without the tag. Entails
  **c230** (not c45 — Lau explicitly says the representation is subpersonal,
  "not a thought in that sense").
- **c234 "A discriminator gates awareness"** — the mechanism: an AI-style
  discriminator distinguishes true perceptions from memory, fantasy, and neural
  noise; its verdict turns sensory information into conscious experience.
  Entails **c233**.
- Theory lists ["c234", "c41"] (c230, c233 inherited). **c41** (functionalism)
  reuse: the page is explicit that Lau's account is functionalist — "a robot or
  computer program could" be conscious.
- Family: Materialism. Blurb: "Seeing something consciously means your brain
  has stamped the signal 'real, not fantasy' — a subpersonal reliability tag,
  like blindsight patients lack."

### 97 — Metzinger's no-self theory
- Page: https://loc.closertotruth.com/theory/metzinger-s-no-self-representational-theory-of-subjectivity
- **c235 "There is no self"** — "nobody ever had or was a self"; only phenomenal
  selves exist, as the content of a transparent self-model, an ongoing process
  referring to nothing. Standalone.
- **c236 "Conscious thought is passive"** — conscious thought is mostly
  subpersonal and non-agentive, lacking veto-control; a stable first-person
  perspective (the epistemic agent model) is a rare, graded achievement.
  Standalone.
- Theory lists ["c235", "c236"].
- Family: Materialism, with caveat: Metzinger is NOT an illusionist — he
  affirms phenomenal consciousness and gives it a causal role in behavior and
  culture. His distinctive claims here concern the self-model, not the reality
  of experience, so neither c53 nor the higher-order lineage is forced.
- Blurb: "Phenomenal experience is real, but the 'self' having it isn't — it's
  a transparent brain-model of a subject."

### 98 — Brown's HOROR
- Page: https://loc.closertotruth.com/theory/brown-s-horor-higher-order-representationalism
- **c237 "Representation of a representation"** — the core: phenomenal
  consciousness = a thought-like, assertoric higher-order representation with
  content "I, myself, am having this representation" — representing the fact
  that representing is going on, not the first-order content directly.
  Misrepresentation-problem response (targets representations, not contents)
  and cognitive-access tie-in are folded into the plain/text rather than
  separate claims. Entails **c45** ("Consciousness needs a higher thought"):
  the CTT page places HOROR in the HOT tradition and calls the representation
  explicitly thought-like.
- Deliberately does NOT entail **c108** (phenomenal character = representational
  content): c108 entails c107 ("No higher-order state needed"), the opposite of
  HOROR's whole point. Brown's "phenomenal character = intentional content of
  the HOR" is close but structurally incompatible with the c108 chain.
- Theory lists ["c237"] (c45 inherited).
- Family: Materialism. Blurb: "Consciousness is a thought about a thought: you
  feel red because your brain represents, as a claim, 'I myself am representing
  red.'"

### 99 — Churchland's eliminative materialism
- Page: https://loc.closertotruth.com/theory/churchland-s-eliminative-materialism
- **c238 "Folk psychology will fall"** — Paul Churchland's signature thesis:
  common-sense psychology is a radically false theory that completed
  neuroscience will displace, not reduce. Entails **c0** (ordinary physics is
  enough): the page frames this as the maximalist physicalist position.
- **c239 "Introspection fabricates phenomenality"** — Frankish's formulation
  quoted on the page: considered as functional processes, the mind is perfectly
  real; considered as an inner realm of phenomenal properties, it is illusory;
  belief in special phenomenal properties is due to introspective illusion.
  Entails **c53** ("There are no real qualia").
- Theory lists ["c238", "c239"] (c0, c53 inherited).
- Family: Materialism. Caveat on the theory record: the CTT page bundles
  several authors (Dennett, Frankish, Graziano) under the eliminativism banner;
  c238 is Churchland's core while c239 follows Frankish's quoted formulation.
- Blurb: "Your everyday ideas about the mind — beliefs, feelings, the inner
  light — are a false folk theory that neuroscience will junk."

### 100 — Ryle's category-dissolution
- Page: https://loc.closertotruth.com/theory/ryle-s-category-dissolution-of-inner-theater-consciousness
- **c240 "The mind is no place"** — the category-mistake diagnosis: mental
  vocabulary names no second private arena (no "ghost in the machine"); it
  describes dispositions, skills, and styles of doing — the University/college
  analogy. Standalone.
- **c241 "No inner eye"** — no introspective faculty scanning a private field;
  no privileged access; self-knowledge comes through observed conduct and
  unstudied talk, the same ways we know others; the systematic elusiveness of
  'I'. Entails **c240**.
- Theory lists ["c241"] (c240 inherited).
- Family: Materialism, with caveat: by landscape convention, not fit — Ryle's
  project is logical-grammatical diagnosis, not materialism, and he conceded
  the sensation analysis did not satisfy him. He is not a cartoon
  eliminativist: the page is explicit he does not deny pains, moods, or
  thoughts occur.
- Blurb: "There's no private theatre inside your head — 'mind' talk describes
  what you can do and how you do it, not ghostly inner objects."

### 101 — Humphrey's user illusion
- Page: https://loc.closertotruth.com/theory/humphrey-s-magical-compelling-user-illusion

  the CTT title is "Magical-Compelling User Illusion").
- Theory lists ["c242"] (c54, c53, c0 inherited — same chain as theory 27,
  Dennett's illusionism).
- Family: Materialism. Blurb: "Your inner life is a magic show the brain
  stages for itself — an evolved trick that makes you feel special and keeps
  you alive."

### 102 — Blackmore's "it's all models"
- Page: https://loc.closertotruth.com/theory/blackmore-s-it-s-all-models
- **c243 "All models are conscious"** — the signature reversal: there are no
  conscious subjects among physical things; every predictive model has
  "something it is like" to be it, while bats, cats, and humans do not. "What
  it is like" is retained but relocated from subjects to models. Standalone
  (deliberately does NOT entail c53 — she keeps phenomenality language).
- **c244 "No conscious/unconscious split"** — the "delusionism" claim: the
  conscious/unconscious division is an illusion; "conscious" just means
  currently connected to the self-model; no NCCs to be found (there are neural
  correlates of perception, memory, model-building — nothing in addition).
  Entails **c243**.
- Theory lists ["c244"] (c243 inherited).
- Family: Materialism, with caveat: the landscape files Blackmore under
  Eliminative/Illusionism, but "all models are conscious" has a
  panpsychist-of-models flavor and she retains "what it is like" language —
  she is a delusionist about the conscious subject, not a standard
  illusionist.
- Blurb: "Nobody is conscious — only the predictive models inside you are.
  'Becoming conscious' is a model joining your self-model."

### 103 — Kammerer's introspective illusionism
- Page: https://loc.closertotruth.com/theory/kammerer-s-introspective-illusionism
- **c245 "Introspection is rigged"** — the distinctive move beyond bare
  illusionism: the TCE theory of phenomenal introspection — we believe in
  phenomenal properties because introspection applies Theoretically-determined
  Concepts of Epistemologically-special states, framing ordinary brain states
  as special; the seeming is produced by our naive epistemology/theory of
  mind. Entails **c53**.
- Theory lists ["c245"] (c53 inherited).
- Family: Materialism, with caveat: Kammerer argues illusionism best defends
  physicalism, but his distinctive claims concern introspection's
  unreliability, not physics — c0 is deliberately not entailed.
- Blurb: "Phenomenal consciousness doesn't exist — it only seems to, because
  introspection is rigged to paint brain states as special."

## Judgment calls and flags for merge review

1. **Positive-claim discipline for eliminativists.** Denials are rendered as
   positive claims about introspection's unreliability ("fabricates",
   "misleads", "rigged") or about replacement (c238). Churchland, Kammerer,
   and Humphrey route through c53; Blackmore deliberately does not. Reviewer
   should sanity-check that Blackmore's non-entailment of c53 is the intended
   reading (she keeps "what it is like" for models) vs. a misfiling.

2. **Humphrey vs. Dennett (theory 27).** Both now route through c54, so they
   share the c54/c53/c0 inheritance chain and differ only in c242 (adaptive
   enchantment). If the product wants the two more distinguishable, split a
   Dennett-specific functional claim later. Acceptable per "identical claims
   acceptable initially."

3. **Ryle's family.** "Materialism" is landscape-convention, recorded in the
   caveat. If a more honest family label (e.g. "Conceptual") is wanted, that's
   a maintainer call — the caveat carries the nuance either way.

4. **Kriegel and Lau vs. c45.** Kriegel (same-state self-representation) and
   Lau (subpersonal tag) deliberately do not entail c45 ("a thought about
   being in that state"); Brown does. A quiz visitor affirming c45 will see
   Brown stay in play while Kriegel/Lau drop — that is the intended
   discrimination, but the reviewer should confirm the c45 wording ("thought")
   is really too strong for Lau's subpersonal tag.

5. **Metzinger isolation.** c235/c236 stand alone with no higher-order
   parent. The CTT landscape files Metzinger under Higher-Order, but his
   self-model-as-ongoing-process claim doesn't logically entail any existing
   higher-order claim, and forcing it would misrepresent the no-self core.
   Reviewer may add a higher-order parent if a suitable general claim emerges.

6. **The two uses of c54.** c54's text mentions "functional pattern" in
   Dennett's sense; Humphrey's enchantment claim entails it as a specialization
   (a user-illusion with adaptive function). If that reads as Dennett-specific,
   consider a standalone Humphrey claim instead of the entail.
